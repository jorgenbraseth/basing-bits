# Warhammer 40,000 11th edition terrain: measurements

The reference sizes for generating 40k terrain pieces. This covers 11th edition only (June 2026 onwards) and the terrain pieces that stand on the table, not the card footprints they sit on. Anything older (10th edition, Pariah Nexus, Sector Imperialis, Sector Mechanicus) is out of date and left out.

**The sizing standard is GW's Battlefields: Armageddon kit.** It is pre-painted (40-70, released 31 Oct 2026). Its sprues are the same as the unpainted 11e Starter Set and Combat Patrol: Battlezone terrain. The Warhammer Event Companion v1.0 draws every layout with this kit and tells players without it to use terrain "close to the same size".

GW publishes no dimensions for any piece, so every number below is labelled:

- **Rule:** stated in the 11e core rules or Event Companion.
- **Measured:** measured by a reviewer, or measured off GW's to-scale layout diagrams.
- **Estimate:** scaled from sprue photos. Use these only until someone measures a real kit.

1" = 25.4 mm.

## Heights the rules care about

These are the numbers a generator must not drift across. Source: 11e core rules sections 13 and 22.05 ([Wahapedia mirror](https://wahapedia.ru/wh40k11ed/the-rules/core-rules/)).

| Height | What it does | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 2" (51 mm) | Vehicles and monsters can move through a Dense feature only where every section their base crosses is 2" or lower | Rule |
| 3" (76 mm) | Dense features block line of sight through enclosed gaps (windows, doors) up to 3" above the ground. The rules note 3" is "the height of the first floor of many terrain features" | Rule |
| 3" (76 mm) | Plunging Fire: +1 BS for a model standing on a section 3" or higher | Rule |
| 4" (102 mm) | Super-heavy walkers ignore sections 4" or lower | Rule |

So floors and ledges should sit at 3" or slightly above, never below. GW sets no height limit for Light pieces. Third-party makers keep them under 2".

## Non-ruin pieces

No heights have been published for the Shock Array, Capacitor Stack, Generator Shrine, Galvanic Pylon or Rockcrete Barricade. The Dense pieces are presumably tall enough to hide infantry. A reviewer mentions "the taller barricades", which suggests the barricades come in two heights.

## Visual style

Imperial Manufactorum / Mechanicus industrial gothic, from the review photos:

- **Ruins:** riveted steel pilaster frames about 1" apart, gothic lancet and arched windows, X-braced lattice bays in the upper storey, louvred vents, heavy riveted double doors with cog and skull motifs, external pipe runs and elbows, gauges, control panels, cable runs, servo-skull niches, broken top edges with rubble.
- **Capacitor Stack:** stacked ribbed cylinders with sculpted lightning, plus a cogitator and servitor unit.
- **Shock Array:** a fence of three rails on posts with lightning arcs between them, nearly flat.
- **Generator Shrine:** a central domed generator with an Imperial aquila and Y-shaped riveted side frames with cable rings.
- **Galvanic Pylon:** A- or Y-frame towers carrying sagging cable bundles. Cable runs are interchangeable, and end caps make a pylon stand alone with severed cables or chain into the Shrine.
- **Rockcrete Barricade:** a slab with an aquila relief and a lattice rail.

## Older GW terrain is too big

Older GW ruins don't fit the 11e layouts. Sector Imperialis storeys are about 5–6" (127–152 mm), against 3" here, and reviewers say older ruins come out "a bit too tall/wide" for the 11e footprints. Don't size pieces from them.

## Still unknown

Caliper measurements of a real kit would settle:

- Large ruin: overall height, wall thickness, window and door openings, floor thickness.
- Small ruin corner height.
- Heights of the Shock Array, Capacitor Stack, Generator Shrine, Galvanic Pylon and Rockcrete Barricade.
